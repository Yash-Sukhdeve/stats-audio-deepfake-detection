Features That Were Excluded and Why\n\nThe v1 feature set contained 214 dimensions. The reduction to 36 was driven by codec robustness analysis. Here are the major exclusions with mathematical justification:\n\n### 5.1 MODGD -- Modified Group Delay (removed in v3)\n\nThe group delay is the negative derivative of the phase spectrum:\n\ntau(omega) = -d phi(omega) / d omega = Re(X'(omega) overline{X(omega)}) / |X(omega)|^2\n\nwhere X'(omega) = F{n * x[n]}. Parametric codecs like G.729 (CS-ACELP) reconstruct the excitation signal entirely from a codebook -- the original phase information is destroyed and replaced by the codebook entry's phase. The formal statement from the codebase: for any LPC-based codec T that reconstructs excitation from a codebook, the pushforward distribution P(phi_{MODGD}(T(X))) bears no necessary relationship to P(phi_{MODGD}(X)). Additionally, the implementation suffered from numerical instability (values exceeding 10^6).\n\n### 5.2 Glottal Features (removed in v2)\n\nGlottal features estimate properties of the voice source signal by inverse-filtering the speech signal through the estimated vocal tract transfer function. The spectral tilt (slope of the glottal source spectrum) is particularly informative for deepfake detection.\n\n**Why excluded:** G.711 applies mu-law or A-law companding:\n\nF(x) = sign(x) ln(1 + mu |x|) / ln(1 + mu), quad mu = 255\n\nThis nonlinear amplitude mapping distorts the spectral slope because it differentially affects high-amplitude and low-amplitude spectral components. The codebase's consolidated analysis documents this as causing \"sign inversion\" in the spectral tilt regression slope -- the feature can flip sign after codec processing, making it worse than useless as a discriminative feature.\n\n### 5.3 Spectral Contrast\n\nSpectral contrast measures the difference between spectral peaks and valleys in each sub-band:\n\nSC_k = log(peak_k) - log(valley_k)\n\n**Why excluded:** Lossy codecs apply sub-band energy quantization (especially MDCT-based codecs like MP3, AAC, Opus-CELT). The quantization step sizes are perceptually weighted, meaning they preserve perceived energy but can significantly alter the peak-to-valley ratio within each sub-band. The contrast ratios are destroyed.\n\n### 5.4 Jitter and Shimmer\n\nJitter = cycle-to-cycle variation in F0 period. Shimmer = cycle-to-cycle variation in amplitude.\n\n**Why excluded:** These micro-perturbation features require precise measurement of individual pitch periods. Patel et al.
