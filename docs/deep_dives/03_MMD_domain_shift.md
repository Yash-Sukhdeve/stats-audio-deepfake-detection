Questions a Mathematician Would Ask\n\n### Q1: \"Is MMD a metric?\"\n\n**Yes**, when H is an RKHS with a characteristic kernel (such as RBF). Specifically, MMD satisfies:\n\n1. **Non-negativity:** MMD(P,Q) >= 0. (Follows from ||.||_H >= 0.)\n2. **Identity of indiscernibles:** MMD(P,Q) = 0 iff P = Q. (This is exactly what \"characteristic\" means.)\n3. **Symmetry:** MMD(P,Q) = MMD(Q,P). (Follows from ||mu_P - mu_Q|| = ||mu_Q - mu_P||.)\n4. **Triangle inequality:** MMD(P,R) <= MMD(P,Q) + MMD(Q,R). (Follows from the triangle inequality of ||.||_H.)\n\nSo MMD is a metric on the space of Borel probability measures with finite kernel moments. It metrizes a topology that is generally weaker than the total variation topology but stronger than convergence in distribution (for characteristic kernels on compact spaces).
